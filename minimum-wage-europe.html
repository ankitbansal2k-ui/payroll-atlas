Minimum wage in Europe 2026

The statutory minimum wage of each country, side by side, as stated by its official or professional source. Amounts are in each country's own currency and are not converted. Each country defines the minimum differently, by hours worked or by the month, and many have age bands or other lower or higher rates, so the "Applies to" column names the rate shown; check the source and the key facts page of the country for the full rules. Looking for another country? See all countries. Monthly figures are not comparable across countries: the number of salary payments per year (12, 13 or 14), working hours and the gross/net basis differ, and the monthly and hourly columns measure different things. Some figures are set by collective agreement or apply to one region; the Applies to column says which. For information only, not legal or tax advice.

No statutory national minimum wage

  • 🇦🇹 Austria: Austria has no statutory minimum wage that applies to all employees; minimum wages are set mainly in sector collective agreements (Kollektivverträge), which cannot be undercut by contract. Most agreements provide at least €1,700 gross, 14 times a year About 95% of employees are covered by a collective agreement (over 800 agreements). Page last updated 12 August 2025. Rate depends on the applicable Kollektivvertrag. · Source: Sozialministerium (BMASGPK) - Mindestlohn in Österreich
  • 🇩🇰 Denmark: Denmark does not have a statutory minimum wage; pay is regulated by collective agreements or negotiated individually Written by the Ministry of Employment, updated 19 May 2026. The page says an individual agreement can stipulate a minimum wage for the sector and that unions publish annual wage statistics. · Source: Business in Denmark (Virk, Ministry of Employment) - Pay
  • 🇫🇮 Finland: There is no minimum wage law in Finland; minimum wages are set in the collective agreement of each sector, and universally binding agreements apply to all employers in the sector If no collective agreement applies, pay is agreed in the employment contract, or the Employment Contracts Act requires a usual and reasonable wage. · Source: Occupational Safety and Health Administration (tyosuojelu.fi) - Pay
  • 🇮🇹 Italy: No statutory national minimum wage (Eurostat, 1 July 2026); minimum pay is laid down by collective agreements for specific sectors Eurostat lists Italy with Denmark, Austria, Finland and Sweden as EU countries without a national minimum wage on 1 July 2026. · Source: Eurostat - Minimum wage statistics
  • 🇳🇴 Norway: No set minimum wage in other sectors; legal minimum wages apply in nine sectors through generalised collective agreements (allmenngjøring), and wages elsewhere are agreed individually or in collective agreements Sector rates (e.g. construction, cleaning, hospitality) are on the source page. The page also presents a new sector, automotive, with minimums from 15 June 2026 (e.g. NOK 208 an hour for unskilled workers aged 18 or over). · Source: Arbeidstilsynet (Norwegian Labour Inspection Authority) - Minimum wage
  • 🇸🇪 Sweden: No statutory minimum wage; wages are often regulated by collective agreements The source page is the Swedish Work Environment Authority's guidance for posted workers. · Source: Swedish Work Environment Authority (Arbetsmiljöverket) - The rights of posted workers in Sweden
