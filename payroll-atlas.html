Income tax & payroll withholding

Skatteetaten

What Skatteetaten's tax deduction card (skattekort) is built from for 2026 — the numbers that decide what comes off gross pay before it hits an employee's account.

Income tax thresholds and bracket tax (trinnskatt) move up

Effective 1 Jan 2026

Skatteetaten raised the personal allowance and shifted every trinnskatt threshold upward for the 2026 income year, alongside a small cut to the national insurance contribution (trygdeavgift) rate.

For the employer
  • No manual recalculation — pull each employee's updated electronic skattekort via A-meldingen before the first 2026 pay run.
  • Check payroll software has loaded the new 2026 trinnskatt and trygdeavgift tables before processing January pay.
For the employee
  • No action needed — the new skattekort is issued automatically by Skatteetaten.
  • Worth checking the card in Skatteetaten's portal if income changed significantly, to avoid a large year-end settlement.
In practiceAn employee earning 550,000 kr a year now has trinnskatt calculated as: 0% on the first 226,100 kr, 1.7% on the next 92,200 kr, and 4.0% on the remainder up to 550,000 kr — each threshold roughly 3–4% higher than in 2025, so slightly more income sits in the lower bands.

Employer's national insurance contribution

Arbeidsgiveravgift

The payroll tax an employer pays on top of gross wages, set by geographic zone based on where the employee's work is registered. Zone boundaries and rates carry over unchanged from 2025.

Zone rates and boundaries unchanged for 2026

Confirmed for 2026

Skatteetaten's 2026 directive keeps the same seven-zone map and rates as 2025, so no re-mapping of employees is required — but it's still worth confirming each employee's registered work municipality is current.

For the employer
  • Confirm each employee's zone in the A-melding is still correct, especially after office moves or new remote-work registrations.
  • Track the 850,000 kr zone Ia exemption per employer, not per employee, if operating there.
  • Finance-sector employers: budget for the additional 5% finansskatt on top of the zone rate.
For the employee
No direct action — arbeidsgiveravgift is paid entirely by the employer and does not appear on a payslip or reduce net pay.
In practiceA Trondheim-based employer (zone III) paying 700,000 kr in gross annual wages to one employee owes 700,000 × 6.4% = 44,800 kr in arbeidsgiveravgift on top of the wage cost. The same salary in Oslo (zone I) costs 98,700 kr in employer's NI — zone matters more here than almost any other variable.

Employment law

Arbeidsmiljøloven

Changes to the Working Environment Act taking effect from 1 January 2026 — HR-facing rather than payroll-facing, but with direct contract and policy consequences.

Psychosocial work environment duties made explicit in law

Effective 1 Jan 2026

Arbeidsmiljøloven §4-3 is amended so the existing "fully satisfactory working environment" duty explicitly covers psychosocial factors: unclear or conflicting job demands, emotional strain from working with people, workload/time-pressure imbalance, and access to support at work. Arbeidstilsynet describes this as a clarification, not a new obligation.

For the employer
  • Map existing HMS risk assessments and work-environment surveys against the four named factors.
  • Document how conflicting demands, emotional strain, workload, and support are systematically assessed and followed up.
For the employee
  • These four factors are now a named, explicit basis for raising a work-environment concern with HR, a safety representative, or Arbeidstilsynet.
In practiceAn HR team's annual work-environment survey should now visibly map to these four named factors, not just physical safety checklists — this is what an Arbeidstilsynet inspection will look for first.
Source: Arbeidstilsynet ↗

Company-set dismissal age limits below 72 are abolished

Effective 1 Jan 2026

Arbeidsmiljøloven §15-13a third paragraph, which let employers set an internal retirement/dismissal age as low as 70 through policy or agreement, is repealed. The statutory floor for age-based termination without the ordinary just-cause test is now 72 for everyone.

For the employer
  • Update employment contracts, staff handbooks, and pension-scheme wording that reference a company retirement age below 72.
  • Any dismissal relying solely on the old clause is no longer lawful — use the ordinary termination rules instead.
For the employee
  • Employment cannot be ended on age grounds alone before turning 72, regardless of what an older contract says.
Source: NHO Service og Handel — summary of 2026 law changes ↗

NAV benefits & grunnbeløp

NAV

Most NAV cash benefits are pegged to the grunnbeløp (G), the base amount NAV re-sets every 1 May. It is the single number that quietly reshapes sick-pay and parental-pay ceilings each year.

Grunnbeløp rises 4.91% from 1 May 2026

Effective 1 May 2026

G moves from 130,160 kr to 136,549 kr. Because sick pay, parental benefit, and disability-related thresholds are all expressed as multiples of G, this single adjustment reshapes several benefit ceilings at once without any separate legislation.

For the employer
  • Update any payroll or HR system that hard-codes the sykepenger qualifying threshold or foreldrepenger ceiling for pay runs from May onward.
  • Continue covering sykepenger in full for the first 16 calendar days of an absence (arbeidsgiverperioden) — this obligation is unaffected by the G change.
For the employee
  • The income needed to qualify for sykepenger or foreldrepenger rises slightly, to 68,275 kr a year from 1 May.
  • The maximum annual income covered by foreldrepenger rises to 819,294 kr — earnings above that are not compensated by NAV unless topped up by an employer scheme.
In practiceThe minimum income needed to qualify for sykepenger (½G) rises to 68,274.5 kr a year, and the ceiling on foreldrepenger (parental benefit, capped at 6G) rises to 819,294 kr a year — payroll systems that hard-code these figures need updating for pay runs from May onward.

Payroll — on the horizon

Watch list

Nothing here changes a pay run today. These are the payroll-related items furthest along in the pipeline, with likely actions sketched out so a team can plan ahead — not yet worth acting on. Reporting-related proposals live in Statutory Reporting below.

Stricter limits on temporary staffing and a "right to full-time" norm

Out for consultation (høring)

The Ministry of Labour and Social Inclusion has circulated a høring proposing changes to arbeidsmiljøloven that would make full-time the default form of employment, add documentation and consultation requirements before a role is advertised as part-time, and extend part-time employees' priority rights ahead of hiring temporary staff (innleie).

Likely employer action, if adopted
  • Justify and document any part-time role in writing before advertising it.
  • Offer available extra hours to existing part-time staff before hiring through a staffing agency.
Likely employee impact, if adopted
  • Part-time employees would gain stronger priority rights to additional hours over external hires.

Public consultation only — no bill has gone to the Storting and no in-force date is set. The scope described here can still change before it becomes law.

Statutory reporting

A-meldingen

Administrative filing obligations — separate from what a payslip shows, but a real compliance workload for payroll teams.

API submission of A-meldingen required by April 2026

Deadline Apr 2026

Skatteetaten requires payroll systems to submit A-meldingen (the combined monthly report covering pay, tax deduction, and arbeidsgiveravgift) via its API by April 2026, moving away from older manual or file-upload submission routes. Forms for third-party information reporting also became newly available on skatteetaten.no from January 2026.

For the employer
  • Confirm the payroll system or payroll provider has implemented API-based A-melding submission ahead of the April 2026 deadline.
  • If using a manual or file-upload process today, plan the switch now rather than close to the deadline.
For the employee
No direct impact — this changes how the employer's system talks to Skatteetaten, not what appears on a payslip.

Watch list

Not yet in force — reporting-shaped proposals to plan around, not act on.

EU Pay Transparency Directive (likelønnsdirektivet)

Awaiting EEA incorporation

Requires employers to disclose pay ranges to job applicants, ban questions about salary history, and — above a headcount threshold — publish regular gender pay-gap reports. EU member states had to transpose it by 7 June 2026, but that deadline does not bind Norway: as an EEA/EFTA country, Norway can only implement it once the directive is added to the EEA Agreement, which the government's own page on the directive confirms has not yet happened.

Likely employer action, once enacted
  • Build defensible, documented pay bands per role ahead of time — this groundwork typically takes longer than the reporting itself.
  • Remove salary-history questions from recruitment processes and prepare to state a pay range in job ads.
Likely employee impact, once enacted
  • Right to ask for the pay range for a role and for average pay by gender for comparable work.

No Norwegian transposition date exists yet — treat any date above as an EU-only reference point, not a Norwegian deadline.

Income tax & payroll withholding

Vero

What Vero's withholding rate (verokortti) is built from for 2026 — the numbers that decide what comes off gross pay before it hits an employee's account.

Tax brackets indexed up, top marginal rate cut, earned-income credit increased

Effective 1 Jan 2026

Vero's 2026 state income tax scale (tuloveroasteikko) raises the lower thresholds by roughly 3.5% for inflation, and the top marginal rate on earned income falls from around 59% to about 52%. The työtulövähennys (earned-income tax credit) maximum rises by €205 to €3,430, with its per-child supplement up €55 to €105 (doubled for single parents), and the municipal basic deduction (kunnallisverotuksen perusvähennys) maximum rises €150 to €4,265.

For the employer
  • No manual recalculation — apply each employee's updated verokortti percentage as Vero issues it, or use the withholding rate already on file if unchanged.
  • Confirm payroll software has loaded Vero's 2026 withholding tables before the first January pay run.
For the employee
  • No action needed for most employees — the new verokortti is calculated automatically by Vero.
  • Worth checking the card via OmaVero if income has changed materially, to avoid an underpayment (jäännösvero) at year-end.
In practiceAn employee whose withholding was based on 2025 rates keeps slightly more net pay in 2026 even at the same salary — the combination of higher earned-income credit and a lower top marginal rate reduces the effective tax rate at most income levels, most noticeably above the point where the old 59% band applied.

Employer's social insurance costs

TyEL · sivukulut

The statutory contributions an employer pays alongside gross wages — pension, health, unemployment, and accident insurance — collectively known as sivukulut. Rates are agreed annually between the pension insurers, the Social Insurance Institution, and the state.

Sivukulut rates confirmed for 2026

Confirmed for 2026

The average TyEL (earnings-related pension) contribution stays split between employer and employee, with the health insurance and unemployment insurance contributions set separately each year. Combined, an employer's total wage cost typically runs 30–35% above gross pay once holiday pay and holiday bonus (lomaraha) are included.

For the employer
  • Budget total employment cost at roughly 1.3–1.35× gross salary once all sivukulut, holiday pay, and holiday bonus are included.
  • Use the pension insurer's (Varma, Elo, etc.) confirmed 2026 TyEL percentage for the specific company, since it varies slightly by insurer's cost-loading.
For the employee
  • The TyEL employee contribution — withheld from gross pay — is 7.30% in 2026, the same flat rate regardless of age.
In practiceOn a 3,500 €/month salary, the employer pays roughly 3,500 × 17.10% ≈ €599 in TyEL, plus roughly 3,500 × 1.91% ≈ €67 in health insurance contribution and smaller unemployment and accident-insurance charges — on top of the €3,500 gross wage, before holiday pay accrual.

Employment law

Työsopimuslaki

Two Employment Contracts Act changes reached force during 2026 — both aimed at lowering the threshold for smaller employers to hire and restructure.

Dismissal threshold on individual grounds is lowered

Effective 1 Jan 2026

The Employment Contracts Act's standard for terminating an individual employee changes from requiring an "asiallinen ja painava syy" (proper and weighty reason) to simply an "asiallinen syy" (proper reason) — removing the "weighty" qualifier that courts had used to set a high bar for individual dismissals.

For the employer
  • Termination decisions still need a documented, proper reason — the bar is lower, not removed — so continue to record performance or conduct issues in writing.
  • Review dismissal and disciplinary templates with legal counsel, since case law interpreting the new "asiallinen syy" standard is still developing.
For the employee
  • Individual dismissals are somewhat easier for an employer to justify than before 2026 — a valid but less exceptional reason can now be sufficient.
Source: Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment — lower threshold for terminating employment ↗

Fixed-term contract rules eased and layoff notice shortened

Effective 1 Apr 2026

Government proposal HE 199/2025 vp (TEM/2026/4) lets an employer offer a fixed-term contract of up to one year without a stated justified reason — either as a person's first contract with that employer, or after a two-year gap since a previous one — capped at three such contracts. Separately, the statutory layoff (lomautus) notice period is shortened from 14 days to 7.

For the employer
  • Track how many unjustified fixed-term contracts have been used with a given employee — the cap is three within the permitted window.
  • Update layoff notice templates and internal timelines to the new 7-day minimum.
For the employee
  • A first fixed-term contract no longer requires the employer to state a specific justified reason.
  • Notice of a layoff can now arrive as little as 7 days before it starts, down from 14.
Source: Ministry of Economic Affairs and Employment — TEM/2026/4 decision page ↗

Kela benefits

Kela

Kela pays sickness and parental allowances that sit alongside employer-paid sick pay, indexed each year to the kansaneläkeindeksi (national pension index) — currently frozen.

Minimum sickness and parental allowances frozen for 2026

Confirmed for 2026

The government's freeze of the kansaneläkeindeksi means Kela's minimum sickness allowance (sairauspäivärahan vähimmäismäärä) and minimum pregnancy/paternity/parental allowance stay at the same euro amount as in 2023–2025, rather than rising with inflation as they normally would.

For the employer
  • No payroll system change needed for the Kela minimum itself — but confirm any employer top-up to statutory sick pay or parental leave is calculated against current, not assumed-increased, Kela minimums.
For the employee
  • An employee relying on the minimum-rate sickness or parental allowance sees no increase in 2026 despite inflation, since the index used to raise it is frozen.
In practiceThe minimum pregnancy, paternity, and parental allowance stays at €31.99 a day in 2026, unchanged since 2023 — an employee on the statutory minimum has effectively seen a real-terms cut over that period as prices have risen.
Source: Kela — Näin Kelan tuet muuttuvat vuonna 2026 ↗

Statutory reporting

Tulorekisteri

Administrative filing obligations built around Finland's Incomes Register — separate from what a payslip shows, but a real compliance workload for payroll teams.

New annual pay-gap-by-group report via the Incomes Register

Effective 2026 reporting year

Employers with at least 100 employees must calculate gender-based pay differences by employee group and report them to the tulorekisteri (Incomes Register) as a separate annual submission, alongside the routine salary information reports the register already collects automatically from payroll runs.

For the employer
  • If at or above 100 employees, confirm the employee-group pay-difference calculation is built and ready before the annual reporting deadline.
  • Where payroll is connected to the tulorekisteri via a technical interface, routine salary reports already generate automatically — this new report is a separate, additional submission.
For the employee
No direct action — this is an employer reporting obligation, though it feeds the same pay-transparency push described below.

Targeting 1 Jan 2027

Income tax & payroll withholding

Skatteverket

What Skatteverket's withholding tables (skattetabeller) are built from for 2026 — the numbers that decide what comes off gross pay before it hits an employee's account.

State tax threshold and grundavdrag rise; prisbasbelopp increases

Effective 1 Jan 2026

The skiktgräns — the income level above which the 20% state income tax applies — rises from 625,800 kr to 643,000 kr for 2026, with a higher brytpunkt (660,400 kr before employer contributions, or 760,400 kr for those 66 and over thanks to an enhanced grundavdrag). The prisbasbelopp, which indexes a wide range of benefits and thresholds, rises from 58,800 kr to 59,200 kr.

For the employer
  • Confirm payroll software has loaded Skatteverket's 2026 skattetabeller before the first January pay run.
  • No separate action for the skiktgräns increase itself — withholding tables already reflect it.
For the employee
  • An employee whose pay sits close to the old 625,800 kr threshold may now fall entirely below the state tax line, or pay it on a smaller slice of income.
  • An employee aged 66+ at the start of the year gets a higher brytpunkt due to the enhanced grundavdrag for older taxpayers.
In practiceAn employee earning 650,000 kr a year now pays 20% state tax only on the 7,000 kr above the new 643,000 kr skiktgräns — in 2025, that slice was a larger 24,200 kr above the old 625,800 kr threshold, so this employee's state tax bill falls even at an unchanged salary.

Employer's contributions

Arbetsgivaravgift

The statutory social contributions an employer pays on top of gross wages — with age-based reliefs that make the effective rate very different for a young hire, a mid-career employee, or a working pensioner.

Standard rate holds at 31.42%, with new relief for young and older workers

Effective through 2026

The standard arbetsgivaravgift stays at 31.42% for employees born 1959–2002. From 1 April 2026 to 30 September 2027, a temporary reduced rate of 20.81% applies to employees born 2003–2007, on pay up to 25,000 kr a month. Separately, from 1 January 2026, employers pay only the 10.21% old-age pension contribution — not the full rate — on pay to employees who turned 67 or older by the start of the year.

For the employer
  • Flag employees born 2003–2007 in payroll so the reduced 20.81% rate applies correctly on their first 25,000 kr of monthly pay from April 2026.
  • Apply the 10.21% pension-only rate to any employee who was 67 or older on 1 January 2026 — a meaningful saving on senior staff costs.
For the employee
Arbetsgivaravgift is paid entirely by the employer and does not appear on a payslip or reduce net pay — though the youth relief is designed to make employers more willing to hire younger staff.
In practiceOn a 22-year-old employee (born 2004) earning 24,000 kr a month from April 2026, the employer pays 24,000 × 20.81% ≈ 4,994 kr in arbetsgivaravgift instead of the standard 24,000 × 31.42% ≈ 7,541 kr — a saving of roughly 2,547 kr a month on that one employee.

Försäkringskassan benefits

Försäkringskassan

Sickness and parental benefits are capped as a multiple of the prisbasbelopp via the sjukpenninggrundande inkomst (SGI) — so the same prisbasbelopp increase that moves tax figures also lifts these ceilings.

Sick pay and parental pay ceiling rises with the prisbasbelopp

Effective 1 Jan 2026

For the employer
  • Update any internal top-up scheme (sjuklön supplements above the statutory floor) that references the prior year's SGI ceiling.

For the employee

Income tax & payroll withholding

Skatteministeriet

2026 is the first year of a major personal tax reform agreed back in December 2023 — it restructures the top of the Danish tax system into three tiers instead of one.

Top tax split into mellemskat, topskat, and a new top-topskat

Effective 1 Jan 2026

The single top tax (topskat) above one threshold is replaced by a three-tier structure: a new 7.5% mellemskat (middle tax) applies to income between 641,200 kr and 777,900 kr (after AM-bidrag), a 15% topskat applies above 777,900 kr, and an entirely new 5% top-topskat applies above 2,592,700 kr a year. The government describes this as halving the top tax for around 280,000 Danes while introducing a new highest tier for very high earners.

For the employer
  • Confirm payroll software has loaded the new three-tier trækprocent calculation for 2026 — this is a structural change, not just an updated threshold.
  • No separate reporting is needed for most employees; SKAT's forskudsopgørelse (preliminary tax assessment) handles the new structure automatically.
For the employee
  • Most employees previously paying topskat see a lower effective rate on income between the old and new thresholds, since mellemskat (7.5%) is lower than the old topskat rate on that slice.
  • An employee earning above roughly 2.6 million kr a year now pays an additional 5% top-topskat on the excess — a genuinely new charge that did not exist in 2025.
In practiceAn employee with 700,000 kr in annual taxable income (after AM-bidrag) now pays mellemskat at 7.5% on the roughly 58,800 kr between 641,200 kr and 700,000 kr — about 4,410 kr — rather than the old, higher topskat rate applying to a larger slice of that income under the pre-2026 single-threshold system.

Employment law

Zákoník práce

The minimum wage rises sharply for 2026 under a new statutory formula, and non-cash wage substitutes are being phased out.

Minimum wage rises to 22,400 Kč under the new valorisation formula

Effective 1 Jan 2026

The minimum wage rises from 20,800 Kč to 22,400 Kč a month (134.40 Kč an hour) — a 1,600 Kč jump, the largest in three years. This is the second year the increase follows a statutory valorisation mechanism introduced by an amendment to the zákoník práce (Act No. 230/2024 Sb.), targeting a minimum-to-average-wage ratio of 47% by 2029.

For the employer
  • Raise any employee paid at or near the old 20,800 Kč minimum to at least 22,400 Kč (134.40 Kč/hour) from the January 2026 pay run.
  • Check that guaranteed-wage tiers (zaručená mzda) for skilled roles, which scale off the minimum wage, are also updated.
For the employee
  • An employee on minimum wage gets an automatic 1,600 Kč/month increase from January 2026, without needing to request it.

Wages can no longer be paid partly in non-cash benefits

Effective 1 Jan 2026

From 2026, it is no longer permitted to pay part of an employee's wage using non-cash instruments such as vouchers, tickets, or cafeteria-style benefit points — wages must be paid in money. Separately, unemployment support (podpora v nezaměstnanosti) now tapers in three steps instead of a flat rate: for those under 52, 80% of prior net pay for the first 2 months, 50% for the next 2, then 40%; for those 52 and over, 80% for 3 months, 50% for the next 3, then 40%.

For the employer
  • Any compensation structure that counted vouchers or benefit points toward the wage itself (rather than as a separate benefit) needs restructuring for 2026.
  • Non-cash benefits can still be offered on top of a fully cash wage — the change bans substituting them for wage, not offering them at all.
For the employee
  • Full wage entitlement must now be paid in money; benefit points or vouchers can only be additional, not a substitute for part of the wage.

What the bedrijfsvoorheffing (payroll withholding tax) is built from for 2026 — the numbers that decide what comes off gross pay before it hits an employee's account.

URSSAF

What Luxembourg's payroll withholding is built from for 2026 — a progressive tax scale plus the country's distinctive automatic wage-indexation system, which moves pay in line with inflation.

First 2026 index tranche triggers a 2.5% wage increase; a second tranche is not expected this year

Effective 1 Jun 2026

Luxembourg's unique wage-indexation system (l'index) automatically raises all wages, salaries, pensions, and social benefits by 2.5% whenever the national consumer price index crosses a trigger threshold — one tranche took effect 1 June 2026, applying economy-wide rather than to any single sector. Per STATEC's 24 June 2026 forecast, no second tranche is expected within 2026 — the next one is now forecast for Q1–Q2 2027, correcting earlier market expectations of a second 2026 increase. The progressive income tax scale itself (up to 42% marginal rate, plus a solidarity surtax) is separately adjusted for inflation roughly every two years.

For the employer
  • Apply the 2.5% index-triggered increase to all wages, salaries, and pay-linked allowances economy-wide — this is not optional or negotiated, it is a statutory adjustment.

  • Every employee receives the 2.5% index adjustment automatically, regardless of sector or individual salary negotiations.
