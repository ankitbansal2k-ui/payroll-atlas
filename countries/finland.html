Minimum wage (no statutory national minimum)

There is no minimum wage law in Finland; minimum wages are set in the collective agreement of each sector, and universally binding agreements apply to all employers in the sector

If no collective agreement applies, pay is agreed in the employment contract, or the Employment Contracts Act requires a usual and reasonable wage.

Source: Occupational Safety and Health Administration (tyosuojelu.fi) - Pay · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Employer statutory social insurance contributions 2026

TyEL pension contribution averages 24.40% of pay, of which the employer share averages 17.10%; unemployment insurance 0.31% or 1.23% depending on total payroll; employer social security (health insurance) contribution 1.91%

Contributions for 2026 per ETK leaflet; the 1.91% social security contribution is payable to the tax authority when the employee is insured in Finland. Workers' compensation and group life premiums vary by insurer and are not covered here.

Source: Finnish Centre for Pension (ETK) - Lakisääteiset sosiaalivakuutusmaksut Suomessa 2026 · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Employee statutory social insurance contributions 2026

TyEL pension 7.30% of pay and unemployment insurance 0.89% of pay, both withheld by the employer; the insured person's health insurance contribution is 1.98%

Per ETK leaflet for 2026. The employer withholds the TyEL and unemployment shares from pay. The 1.98% (medical care and daily allowance contributions) is the insured person's health insurance contribution, payable to the tax authority.

Source: Finnish Centre for Pension (ETK) - Lakisääteiset sosiaalivakuutusmaksut Suomessa 2026 · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

State income tax scale on earned income 2026

12.64% up to EUR 22,000; 19.00% on EUR 22,000-32,600; 30.25% on 32,600-40,100; 33.25% on 40,100-52,100; 37.50% above EUR 52,100 (taxable earned income)

State tax only; the source says municipal and church tax are levied separately on earned income. Tax credits are not covered. The source gives tax at each lower limit (EUR 2,780.80; 4,794.80; 7,063.55; 11,053.55).

State tax scale indexed, top marginal rate cut to 52%, earned-income credit raised

Status: In force 1 Jan 2026

For 2026 the lower thresholds of the state income tax scale were indexed by about 3.5%, and the top marginal tax rate was lowered to 52% (previously about 59%); this is the overall marginal rate as stated by Vero, not the state scale's top band (37.50%, before municipal and church tax). The maximum earned-income tax credit (työtulovähennys) rose by €205 to €3,430, and its child supplement rose by €55 to €105 per child.

For the employer

  • Withholding is driven by each employee's tax card - make sure 2026 tax cards from Vero are loaded for the first 2026 pay run.

For the employee

  • Most earners see lower withholding in 2026; higher earners benefit most from the top marginal rate cut.

Source: Vero (Finnish Tax Administration) - Muutokset veroperusteisiin 2026 · Payroll impact: Medium: new rates, thresholds or bands to load and check · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026 · Updated 4 October 2026

Pay transparency bill: pay-gap reporting via the Incomes Register

Status: Draft — not yet law 1 Jan 2027 Proposed from 1 Jan 2027 High impact

On 9 July 2026 the government proposed amendments to equal-pay legislation implementing the EU pay transparency directive, with entry into force proposed for 1 January 2027. Employers regularly employing at least 100 workers would report gender pay-gap data through the Incomes Register.

For the employer

  • If you regularly employ 100+ workers, prepare to report pay-gap data through the Incomes Register.

For the employee

  • Pay-gap information on your employer becomes available through the new reporting.

Government proposal - subject to parliamentary approval.
