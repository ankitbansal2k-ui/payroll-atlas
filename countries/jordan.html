Social security contribution rates: employer 14.25%, employee 7.5%

Status: Ongoing

Under the Social Security Law No. 1 of 2014, firms pay and deduct monthly contributions to the Social Security Corporation. The law sets: old-age, disability and death insurance 11% paid by the firm and 6.5% deducted from the insured; work injury insurance 2% paid by the firm; maternity insurance 0.75% paid by the firm; unemployment insurance 0.5% paid by the firm and 1% deducted from the insured. Added together these are 14.25% for the employer and 7.5% for the employee. The maximum wage on which contributions are calculated is JOD 3,000, adjusted to inflation each January.

For the employer

  • Pay 14.25% of the insured wage (11% + 2% + 0.75% + 0.5%) to the Social Security Corporation each month, and also pay the insured's share that you deduct.
  • Deduct 7.5% from the insured wage (6.5% + 1%).
  • Contributions run month by month from the first day of employment until it ends; a part month counts as a full month.
  • Cap the contribution base at the maximum wage (JOD 3,000, adjusted each January); check the current figure with the Corporation.

For the employee

  • 7.5% of the insured wage is deducted by the employer (6.5% old-age, disability and death; 1% unemployment).

In practice: Insured wage of JOD 1,000 (below the JOD 3,000 maximum): employer contribution JOD 142.50 (14.25%), employee deduction JOD 75.00 (7.5%), total JOD 217.50.

The law text published by the Corporation includes amendments up to 16 April 2023; PwC Worldwide Tax Summaries (reviewed 5 July 2026) also gives 14.25% and 7.5%. These are the rates for ordinary insured workers: voluntarily insured persons pay 17.5% (Article 59(a)(3)) and workers in agricultural holdings have only work-injury and maternity cover (Article 59(d)(1)). Whether the law was amended after 16 April 2023 was not checked.

Source: Social Security Corporation - Social Security Law No. 1 of 2014 and its amendments (to 16 April 2023) · Payroll impact: Low: little or no payroll configuration needed · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 12 October 2026

Social security: payment within 15 days of the following month; worker data reporting dates

Status: Ongoing

The Social Security Law requires a firm to pay the Social Security Corporation the contributions it deducts from its insured workers and the contributions it pays for them during the first 15 days of the month after they fall due. Default interest of 1% per month applies to any overdue contribution. The firm must also give the Corporation detailed data (names and wages) on workers: those employed in January by the end of March each year; new starters by the end of the month after they start; leavers by the end of the month after service ends. A fine of 1% of the due contributions applies for every month the data is not provided.

For the employer

  • Pay each month's contributions in the first 15 days of the following month.
  • Send the January worker list (names and wages) by the end of March each year.
  • Report new starters and leavers by the end of the month after the start or termination.
  • Late payment: default interest of 1% per month on the overdue amount. Missing data: a fine of 1% of the due contributions per month.

In practice: JOD 1,000 of contributions left unpaid after the 15-day window: default interest of JOD 10 (1%) for each month it stays overdue.

If the data is missing or does not match the real situation, the Corporation may calculate contributions by its own estimate and the firm must pay on that basis.
