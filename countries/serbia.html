Employer social contributions (salaried employees)

Pension and disability insurance 10%; health insurance 5.15%

Valid from 1 January 2026

Law Art. 44: total health rate 10.3%; when contributions are paid from and on the base, health is 5.15%. The 5.15% employer share is a reading of "from and on the base" (10.3% / 2): the law does not label it employer in so many words.

Contribution base limits for 2026: minimum RSD 51,297, maximum RSD 732,820

Status: In force 1 Jan 2026

The Republic Fund for Pension and Disability Insurance (PIO (Republic Fund for Pension and Disability Insurance)) states that, under data published in Official Gazette 112/25, the lowest monthly contribution base is RSD 51,297 and the highest monthly base is RSD 732,820 from 1 January to 31 December 2026. The highest annual base is RSD 8,793,840.

For the employer

  • If the wage paid is lower than the lowest monthly base, contributions are paid on that lowest base valid on the day of payment.
  • If the wage paid is higher than the highest monthly base, contributions are paid on that highest base.

For the employee

  • Contributions on pay above RSD 732,820 a month are capped at the highest base.

In practice: At the 24% combined pension and disability rate: RSD 51,297 x 24% = RSD 12,311.28; RSD 732,820 x 24% = RSD 175,876.80 (PIO table, persons under Article 15).

PIO page: lowest base is 35 percent and highest base five times the average monthly wage for October 2024 to September 2025, published in Official Gazette 112/25 (per the page). The page's headline table and 24% example are for persons insured under Article 15 of the pension law; the lowest/highest base sections cite Articles 37 and 42 of the Law on Contributions generally.

Minimum wage set at RSD 405 an hour for 2027

Status: Upcoming 1 Jan 2027

The Government adopted a decision setting the minimum price of labour for January-December 2027 at RSD 405 per working hour, excluding tax and social insurance contributions, an increase of 9.2 percent. The monthly minimum wage will be RSD 70,470.

For the employer

  • Pay at least RSD 405 per working hour (before tax and contributions) from January 2027.
  • Update pay rates for staff on the minimum wage before the January 2027 payroll.

For the employee

  • Employees on the minimum wage are entitled to at least RSD 70,470 a month from January 2027, at the rate of RSD 405 per working hour.

In practice: RSD 70,470 a month / RSD 405 per hour = 174 working hours.

Source is a Government news item dated 20 August 2026 on the budget revision; it says the decision was adopted at the session. The Official Gazette text was not checked.

Non-taxable part of salary rises to RSD 37,369 from 2027

Status: Upcoming 1 Jan 2027

The Law amending the Personal Income Tax Law (Official Gazette 80/2026, in force from 8 September 2026) sets the monthly reduction of the salary tax base for a full-time employee at RSD 37,369. The amending law applies from 1 January 2027. Salary tax stays at 10 percent.

For the employer

  • From January 2027 reduce the salary tax base by RSD 37,369 a month for an employee working full time, and proportionally for part-time work.
  • Where a person works full time for two or more employers, each employer applies the reduction in proportion to the working time, with the total reduction at RSD 37,369 a month.

For the employee

  • The part of salary that is not taxed rises to RSD 37,369 a month for full-time work from 1 January 2027.

In practice: Gross salary RSD 100,000: (RSD 100,000 - RSD 37,369) x 10% = RSD 6,263.10 salary tax.

Rests only on the Ministry of Finance consolidated text, which it labels unofficial (Official Gazette 80/2026 of 31 August 2026 was not retrieved). Whether Article 15a falls under the carve-out for provisions applying only from 2028 could not be determined from that text; the Minister of Finance's statement implies 2027. The Government page https://www.srbija.gov.rs/vest/994138/vlada-predlozila-da-minimalac-bude-70470-dinara.php also says the non-taxable part rises by 9.2%, like the minimum wage.

New-hire contribution refund extended to 2028; new conditions from 2028 (unofficial Ministry text)

Status: Upcoming 1 Jan 2027

The Law amending the Law on Mandatory Social Insurance Contributions (Official Gazette 80/2026, in force from 8 September 2026) extends the refund of contributions for new hires in Article 45 to wages paid up to 31 December 2028, from 1 January 2027, according to the Ministry of Finance's unofficial consolidated text. The refund covers part of the contributions paid (employee and employer share) on the wage of a newly hired person. The same text applies the new conditions in Article 1 paragraphs 2 to 12 only from 1 January 2028.

For the employer

  • Refund rate: 65% of contributions for 1 to 9 new hires, 70% for 10 to 99 and 75% for 100 or more.
  • A new hire must have been registered as unemployed with the National Employment Service without interruption for at least six months before the job started.
  • From 1 January 2028, according to the Ministry text: unemployed people under 24 registered in the Youth Guarantee programme are exempt from the six-month rule; the relief runs for at most 12 months from the hire and only if the hire raises the employer's net number of employees against the average of the previous 12 months, under state aid rules.
  • From 1 January 2028, according to the Ministry text: combined with the corresponding income tax relief, the relief may not exceed 50% of the wage costs of the people hired or EUR 5.5 million per employer a year.

For the employee

  • No direct effect on the employee's own contributions.

Source is the Ministry of Finance consolidated text, which it labels unofficial; the Official Gazette text was not retrieved. The current wording gives 31 December 2026 as the end date (Tax Administration consolidated law: https://purs.gov.rs/upload/media/2025/12/15/760519/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD_%D0%BE_%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B0_%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%BD%D0%BE_%D1%81%D0%BE%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%98%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BD%D0%BE_%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%9A%D0%B5_-_%D1%83_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8_%D0%BE%D0%B4_01012026.pdf).
