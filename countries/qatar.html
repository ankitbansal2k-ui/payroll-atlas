Minister of Labour Decision No. 50 of 2026 sets fixed WPS wage due dates

Status: In force 9 Sep 2026

Minister of Labour Decision No. 50 of 2026, published in the Official Gazette on 8 September 2026 and effective 9 September 2026, amends the Wage Protection System (WPS (Wage Protection System (also written Wages Protection System))) rules: wages of monthly- or annually-paid employees are due on the first day of each calendar month, other workers are paid every two weeks, and employers must transfer wages to a bank or financial institution in Qatar within seven days of the due date via the WPS.

For the employer

  • Pay monthly/annual-salary employees' wages due on the 1st of each calendar month and transfer them through the WPS within seven days of that date.
  • Realign payroll calendars and bank instruction timelines to the fixed due date.
  • Failure to comply with the WPS rules may result in fines and suspension of Ministry of Labour services.

For the employee

  • Wages now have a fixed due date - the first day of each calendar month for monthly-paid employees.

Qatar Labour Law amended: Ministry can suspend services of employers over wage violations

Status: In force 25 Jun 2026

Law No. 9 of 2026 (dated 11 June 2026, published in Official Gazette No. 11 on 25 June 2026) amends the Labour Law (Law No. 14 of 2004). New Article 146 bis lets the Minister of Labour suspend all or some of an employer's applications and transactions with the Ministry for a breach of the law, and extend the suspension to the employer's related establishments where the breach is repeated in the same year or collective disputes arise from late payment of wages. A worker harmed by a breach of Article 66 (reported by law firms as the wage-payment provision) may ask to change employer or end the contract, with the employer bearing the cost of return to the worker's home country.

For the employer

  • Pay wages on time: late wages in a collective dispute can lead to suspension of Ministry of Labour transactions, extended to related establishments.
  • The suspension does not cover attestation of employment contracts or renewal of work licences and residence permits of the workers of the establishments concerned.
  • Under new Article 146 bis/1 the Minister may publish the names of establishments that repeatedly breach certain provisions, including Article 66.

For the employee

  • A worker affected by an employer's breach of Article 66 can request a change of employer or end the contract, with return travel costs borne by the employer.

Official text read on the Al Meezan Qatar legal portal (Arabic), which marks displayed articles as non-official; the Official Gazette copy governs. The law says only that it is published in the Official Gazette, so the 25 June 2026 effective date is the publication date. Law firms report that ministerial decisions are expected, and the law changes no wage rates or filings.
