Minimum wage (minimální mzda)

22,400 Kč per month or 134.40 Kč per hour for a 40-hour working week, from 1 January to 31 December 2026

Valid from 1 January 2026

Pro-rated for shorter agreed weekly hours. Set by MPSV notice No. 356/2025 Sb. (coefficient 0.434 x forecast average wage of 51,497 Kč, rounded).

Source: MPSV - Minimální mzda · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Employer social security contributions

Social security 24.8% of assessment base (sickness insurance 2.1%, pension insurance 21.5%, state employment policy contribution 1.2%)

Higher employer rates: 27.8% for hazardous work (to rise to 28.8% in 2027 and 29.8% from 2028) and 29.8% for paramedics and company fire units. Employer discounts not covered. Health insurance is a separate fact.

Source: ČSSZ - Sazby pojistného · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Employee social security contributions

Social security 7.1% of assessment base (sickness insurance 0.6%, pension insurance 6.5%)

Health insurance is a separate fact. Discounts (e.g. for working pensioners) and other special cases are not covered.

Source: ČSSZ - Sazby pojistného · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Maximum assessment base for social security 2026

2,350,416 Kč per year (48 times the 2026 average wage of 48,967 Kč) for employees and self-employed

Valid from 1 January 2026

Applies to social security contributions only. For health insurance see the separate assessment base fact.

Source: ČSSZ - Přehled nejdůležitějších údajů pro sociální zabezpečení v roce 2026 · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Personal income tax rates 2026 (employment income)

15% on the part of the tax base up to 36 times the average wage (1,762,812 Kč a year); 23% on the part above that limit

Valid from 1 January 2026

Monthly withholding advance uses 3 times the average wage (146,901 Kč): 15% up to it, 23% above it. Average wage 2026 is 48,967 Kč. Tax credits (slevy na dani) and deductions not covered.

Health insurance premium (zdravotní pojištění), employees

13.5% of the assessment base: the employer pays two thirds (9%) and the employee one third (4.5%)

The employer pays the whole premium to the health insurer and withholds the employee share from pay without consent. Due from the 1st to the 20th day of the following month.

Source: VZP ČR - 2.4. Plátce pojistného – zaměstnavatel · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Health insurance assessment base limits, employees

Minimum base is the minimum wage (22,400 Kč a month from 1 January 2026), so the minimum premium is 3,024 Kč; there is no maximum assessment base (abolished)

Valid from 1 January 2026

Exceptions apply to some employees, for example those for whom the state also pays the premium; the minimum is reduced in proportion for part-month employment.

Source: VZP ČR - Vyměřovací základ a výpočet pojistného · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Minimum wage rises to 22,400 Kč

Status: In force 1 Jan 2026

From 1 January to 31 December 2026, the minimum wage for a 40-hour working week is 22,400 Kč a month or 134.40 Kč an hour (MPSV communication No. 356/2025 Sb.). For shorter agreed hours it is pro-rated, e.g. 11,200 Kč a month for 20 hours a week.

For the employer

  • Raise any employee below 22,400 Kč (134.40 Kč/hour, pro-rated for part-time) from the January 2026 pay run.

For the employee

  • Minimum-wage workers get a higher floor from January 2026.

Source: MPSV - Minimální mzda · Payroll impact: Medium: new rates, thresholds or bands to load and check · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026

Benefits that replace part of the wage lose their tax exemption

Status: In force 2026 High impact

From 2026 leisure and health non-cash benefits stay tax-exempt only when they are given on top of the agreed wage. Benefits that replace part of the salary lose the exemption and are taxed as employment income.

For the employer

  • Review pay packages where benefits replace part of the agreed wage: those benefits must be taxed as employment income.

For the employee

  • Benefits given in place of part of your salary are taxed like salary; benefits on top of your wage can stay tax-exempt.

Source: Expats.cz - How Czechia's 2026 tax laws and changes will hit employees and companies · Payroll impact: High: a new process, system, filing or calculation method · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 30 September 2026 · Updated 3 October 2026

Sickness benefit reduction thresholds raised for 2026

Status: In force 1 Jan 2026

ČSSZ states that the reduction thresholds for the daily assessment base used for sickness insurance benefits rise in 2026: 1,633 Kč (counted at 90%), 2,449 Kč (60%) and 4,897 Kč (30%).

For the employer

  • Use the 2026 thresholds when calculating wage compensation (náhrada mzdy) and sickness benefits.

For the employee

  • Sickness benefits for higher earners are calculated from higher thresholds.

Source: ČSSZ - Přehled nejdůležitějších údajů pro sociální zabezpečení v roce 2026 · Payroll impact: Low: little or no payroll configuration needed · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026

Jednotné měsíční hlášení zaměstnavatele (JMHZ) introduced

Status: In force 1 Jan 2026 Phased from 1 Jan 2026 High impact

Act No. 323/2025 Sb. on the single monthly employer report (JMHZ (Jednotné měsíční hlášení zaměstnavatele (Czech) - unified (single) monthly employer report)) took effect on 1 January 2026. It lets employers meet their reporting and record-keeping duties to several state authorities through one electronic submission. Employer obligations take effect in three main phases: 1 January 2026, 1 April 2026 and 1 July 2026.

For the employer

  • Confirm payroll software supports JMHZ submissions and the obligations applying in each phase.

For the employee

  • No direct payslip impact.

Source: ČSSZ - Co je JMHZ · Payroll impact: High: a new process, system, filing or calculation method · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026

EU Pay Transparency Directive - phased Labour Code amendment

Status: Draft — not yet law 1 Jan 2027 Mostly from 1 Jan 2027 High impact

The draft amendment to the Labour Code, published on 27 March 2026, transposing the directive foresees phased entry into force: most provisions are expected to take effect from 1 January 2027, while regular pay-gap reporting, the related pay assessment and corrective measures, and employees' right to request written pay information are expected from 1 January 2028.

For the employer

  • Prepare objective pay structures for the 2027 core obligations, and pay-gap data for 2028 reporting.

For the employee

  • Right to request written information on own pay expected from 2028.

Source: EY Czech Republic - Transparentnost odměňování, 1. část · Payroll impact: High: a new process, system, filing or calculation method · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026
