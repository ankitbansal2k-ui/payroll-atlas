Guaranteed average minimum monthly income (GGMMI / GMMI, CAO no. 43)

€2,233.61 gross per month for employees aged 18 and over, from 1 July 2026 (€2,189.81 from 1 April 2026)

Valid from 1 July 2026

Set by collective labour agreement no. 43 of the National Labour Council, as amended by CAO no. 43/18 of 24 March 2026. The 1 July 2026 amount is the indexed figure after the pivot index was exceeded at the end of June 2026.

Employer social security contributions (private sector, basic and wage moderation)

Basic employer rate 24.92% of gross pay (private sector); plus wage moderation contribution of 5.67% of pay plus 5.67% of the employer contributions due; extra 1.60% (1.69% with wage moderation) for employers with on average 10 or more employees

From the RSZ/NSSO DmfA instructions 2026/3. The tax shift lowers the basic rate to 19.88% for workers in categories 1 and 3 of the structural reduction. Not covered: manual-worker holiday contributions, special contributions, reductions.

Source: RSZ/NSSO (socialsecurity.be) - Administratieve instructies RSZ 2026/3: De socialezekerheidsbijdragen · As of 4 October 2026 · Checked 4 October 2026

Employee social security contributions (worker share by branch)

Pensions 7.50%; health care 3.55%; sickness and invalidity benefits 1.15%; unemployment 0.87% (these four shares add up to 13.07% of gross quarterly pay)

Worker shares from the RSZ/NSSO instructions 2026/3, applied to gross quarterly pay. The 13.07% total is the sum of the four listed shares (computed here, not stated by the page).

GMMI indexed to €2,233.61 from 1 July 2026

Status: In force 1 Jul 2026

On 1 July 2026 the guaranteed average minimum monthly income (GMMI (Gewaarborgd gemiddeld minimummaandinkomen (GGMMI, Dutch) - guaranteed average minimum monthly income)) is indexed after the pivot index was exceeded at the end of June 2026, raising it from €2,189.81 (base amount from 1 April 2026) to €2,233.61 a month for workers aged 18 and over.

For the employer

  • Raise any employee aged 18+ paid at or near the GMMI to at least €2,233.61 a month from 1 July 2026, and correct the July 2026 pay run if it was processed at the old amount.

For the employee

  • Workers on or near the guaranteed minimum get a further increase from 1 July 2026.

The 1 April 2026 step added €35 (non-indexed) to reach €2,189.81; the 1 July 2026 indexation brings it to €2,233.61 (CAO no. 43/18).

Source: Nationale Arbeidsraad (NAR) - Tabel cao-bedragen bijgewerkt op 01.07.2026 (cao nr. 43/18) · Payroll impact: Medium: new rates, thresholds or bands to load and check · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026 · Updated 4 October 2026

"Loon-datatransfer" tool to replace DmfA

Status: Upcoming 1 Jan 2027 Voluntary from 2027 High impact

As part of RSZ (RSZ (Dutch) / ONSS (French) - Belgium's national social security office)'s e-gov modernisation, a new "loon-datatransfer" (wage data transfer) tool will be available for voluntary use and testing from 1 January 2027, become mandatory from 1 January 2028, and the DmfA (Déclaration multifonctionelle / multifunctionele Aangifte - multifunctional declaration) submission itself will be abolished from 1 January 2030. RSZ began sending informational letters to employers about the transition from 28 May 2026.

For the employer

  • Watch for RSZ's informational letters and confirm payroll software providers have a roadmap for the new tool well before the 2028 mandatory date.
  • Consider voluntary testing from 2027 rather than waiting for the 2028 deadline, given a full DmfA phase-out follows in 2030.

For the employee

  • None - this replaces the technical reporting channel between employer and RSZ, not payslip content.

Source: UNIZO - Nieuwe tool voor loon- en arbeidstijdgegevens · Payroll impact: High: a new process, system, filing or calculation method · Last verified 1 October 2026 · Added 8 September 2026

EU Pay Transparency Directive - Belgium misses 7 June 2026 deadline

Status: Draft — not yet law Transposition delayed High impact

Directive (EU) 2023/970 had to be transposed by 7 June 2026, and Belgium will not meet that deadline. The government has asked the European Commission not to open infringement proceedings for late transposition for six months.

For the employer

  • Start building job-level pay-band documentation now - this groundwork is needed regardless of when the Belgian law lands.

For the employee

  • Specific national rights depend on the Belgian transposition law, which is not yet adopted.
